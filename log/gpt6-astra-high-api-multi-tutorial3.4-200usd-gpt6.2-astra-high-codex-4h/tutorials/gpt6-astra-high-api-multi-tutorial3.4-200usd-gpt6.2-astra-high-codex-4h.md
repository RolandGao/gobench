9×9 is a fight about **who gets the larger half**. You have time. Use the opening tree on a match. Otherwise read privately; don't automatically follow their last stone.

1. **Audit life BEFORE searching for a big move.** Re-anchor on the printed board (A B C D E F G H J = 1…9, H next to J), not a remembered shape. Follow orthogonally connected strings around bends; a diagonal is two strings. Classify important groups, including surrounding walls: *independently alive*, *firmly joined to life*, *escape/base still open*, or *surrounded*? On all surrounded/contact strings count the DISTINCT empty neighbor coordinates. In your scratch reasoning identify the endangered strings by anchor, liberty SET if ≤4, and possible links/eye pockets. Do this for BOTH colors. A long wall without eyes or an exit is unsettled. Reassess when the exterior is blocked.
2. **Null move test.** If you ignore them, name their worst cut, seal, atari or *placement*. Read how you'd live then. E.g. a six-stone wall reaching the edge whose only liberties are three adjacent second-line points and no other exit can die; a 4-point tenuki can cost 30. Conversely if their cut is caught in a net, both halves live, or only a worthless tail dies, DON'T pay for an unnecessary solid connection. Also ask what threat of YOURS they just didn't answer.
3. **Compare lines, not slogans.** Generate coordinates for vital local offense/defense, countercapture, expansion/containment, and the best DIFFERENT boundary. Include their vital point. For your top 2–3 mentally place each, merge adjacent friendly strings, take enemy captures first, then form the new liberty set (count shared points once; only removed points touching the new string are its liberties). Switch sides: find their BEST refutation, including capture/throw-in or ignoring it. Read my reply, their reply; follow forced ataris to capture, escape or connection. Redraw the little post-move position if necessary. Compare survival, next free move and area in the worst credible branch. Veto a line losing a big chain. If all safe lines lose on points instead seek a real complication.
4. **Act.** Recheck the actual board, color and legal coordinate. Remember your intended continuation. Follow through if they answer as predicted unless a real new threat outranks it; otherwise reconsider. Output only the move.

**Phase changes FAST.** Take supported central and third/fourth-line positions; fence very low stones in from outside. Don't scatter disconnected invasions. Stop playing opening moves once fighting begins, often by move 10. More center stones behind a wall may score NOTHING.

**The perimeter scan (especially important).** Resist overconnecting or pushing from behind. In reviewed games many best moves from turn 8 onward were on the SECOND line; by turns 20–35 most big points were on lines 1 or 2, many far from last move and **not adjacent to any friendly stone**. Trace B/H files and rows 2/8, then the adjacent edge. FIND candidates; what if THEY get here first?
- Hane *around* the leading enemy stone, rather than a plain extension alongside. E.g. a black D3 next to white C3 near the bottom: consider Black C2, not just D2. It reaches into their side and threatens a slide or further hane. Read whether White can cut at D2 and actually keep the cutter. First-line hane/descent can decide the game.
- Undercut a loose base: the second-line point BELOW a gap between two opposing third-line stones. E.g. enemy C5 and C7 don't own the left edge by fiat; B6 can threaten to turn either way, break their connection or spoil their base. Works best with open edge space or support nearby, not into an unbreakable wall. Look for knight's moves, diagonal slides and monkey jumps underneath ends. A point adjacent only to ENEMY stones, but backed one step away by your wall, can be a very powerful attachment, clamp, or eye-stealing placement. Conversely preempt THEIR slide or invasion before connecting near stones that were already safe.
- Where a wall has TWO ends, check the other one! One end may be settled; the other allows containment, crossing underneath, or a whole corner. A secure fifth-line wall can enclose 5×9=45, enough for Black. Four columns ×9=36 isn't enough even for White. Estimate jagged strips row by row. If you're building a safe but losing strip, stop politely pushing; take the bend, cut at the seam, or enter the still-open far side now. If already ahead, close their profitable entry rather than gambling on a kill.

**The strength to ignore.** Before connecting, let them cut in your mind. Can the cutter survive, and do both halves need each other? If not, tenuki or press forward. If chased, escape *ahead* into space or link up/make a base, not just one liberty farther down a corridor. If an escape needs several moves while the attacker builds territory, give up the tail and take sente. But keep pivotal cutting stones if their capture would save two enemy groups. Attack to gain something even if they live: push them toward a strong wall, not toward their friends, take the outside, settle your own group, or get two threats (miai).

Tactics: at two liberties, test both ataris and a net one step back covering both exits. If the forced extension would have only two liberties again, try continuing the staircase (ladder) all the way; it fails if the runner connects, counter-captures, or gets 3+ liberties. Don't atari them into good shape. Before extending your own ataried stones look for an *adjacent countercapture*: the removed stones may supply more liberties or connect you. Even with no capture, test a counter-atari, especially directly underneath their cutter on the first line. Sacrifice the tail if that seals off the whole invasion. Snapback test: after capturing, can they retake your enlarged string on its ONLY liberty? In a crosscut check everyone's liberties before the obvious atari: extend the crucial stones, leave the less important side light. A diagonal pair has two bridging points; being cut at one may let you connect at the other. One-space jump, knight's move, diagonal, bamboo joint are only connections if the wedge cannot survive. During a capturing race count each string's outside vs shared liberties; fill outside liberties first unless reading shows a faster forcing throw-in, approach move, eye, or capture. Don't fill a shared liberty in seki when the opponent has the last capture. In ko, make a threat they really must answer or trade for more elsewhere.

**Life test cuts both ways.** Before you tenuki from a "dead invasion", inspect the chains that enclose it. If ONE enclosing wall dies, the prey revives: both groups and the empties swing. Resolve tight liberty races before small endgame. Conversely their eye-poor fourth-line fence may die from UNDERNEATH: test a 2–3 placement before conceding the corner. Try the point that both makes eyes for you AND denies their eyes/connection, especially in the corner/second line. A two-liberty captor of many 'dead' stones may need help much more than the prey. If neither player can safely fill a last shared liberty, leave the seki intact.

Life-and-death: Does the enclosed group have TWO independent eyes, room to form them, a connection out, or a liberty race against the wall? Locate the exact point that would create the second eye or destroy it. One large chamber is not automatically two: defender splits it, attacker occupies the division point. Straight/bent three → middle, T four → junction, bulky five → vital center. Straight/bent four (not bent four trapped in the corner) usually has two equivalent division points for the defender. A false eye can be filled while something surrounding it is in atari; inspect diagonal defects (one bad diagonal can spoil an edge eye). Never fill your last real eyes for 'territory'. An invader with no base or way out need not be captured at once; block escape or kill eye shape, then leave it until cleanup. For your own invasions plan where both eyes come from or where to escape; a one-stone unsupported landing inside strong sealed walls probably just dies. Invade earlier, at the *open seam*, third line under a loose high framework; if deep entry is too risky play at the sector boundary and reduce from the outside.

Closing: compare the swing on both flanks. Cash sente (severe follow-up) before small gote. Preempt THEIR profitable forcing move. Solidifying safe interior gains zero while real gaps remain. Take safe dame even for 1 area; no pass bonus. Exact strict score: living OR dead stones left on board each count 1; flood each orthogonally connected EMPTY region: touches only B = all Black, only W = all White, touches both or neither = neutral. Add White 7. Black's margin = 2×Black area + neutral − 88. Without neutrals, 45 Black wins, 44 draws; 38 White wins, 37 draws. Captured prisoners do not count. Safe plays inside your own territory don't reduce your score but do cost a turn. Dead enemy stones MAY NEUTRALIZE an entire empty component; physically capture them safely before two passes; also close any leaks. If they just passed, compute actual final result **as printed** (row totals can help): pass immediately if winning. Secure a draw if no safe improvement. If losing don't consent; seek a cut, ko, squeeze, or a legal placement neutralizing their largest empty region until captured. Never resign.

**Handoff from the tree.** What did your last play threaten? Translate these examples to the ACTUAL board. In any unsettled corner fight, for either color, explicitly test BOTH 2–1 points: A2/B1, A8/B9, J2/H1, J8/H9. One can steal the only eye or secure the base.
- Black E5 vs C5/D5: after the left/upper boundary forms they often invade the **lower right**. An enclosed W G3/H3/G2/J2 with H2 and the bottom edge empty: Black H1 *under* the stones can destroy both potential eyes; routine G1 lets WHITE play H1 and split eyes. With W H1 instead of J2, B J2 is often the vital placement/connection. Before saving a lone ataried B G3 against a W G2 wedge, test B G1 counter-atari from below. In the D5 line with B B6/C7 against W B7/B8, don’t keep pushing C8 without reading **B C6**: this elbow connects the cutting stones and prevents W C6. If W takes C6, test B5 for the crucial B6 cutter before rescuing C7.
- To LIVE on the edge, find a potential eye with three friendly neighbors. Test its FOURTH neighbor rather than filling it. E.g. after White E5/D4 D5/C5 crosscuts, W F2/G3/H2 around empty G2, B H3/J2: test W **G1**, which encloses G2 and gives room toward H1, even though it doesn't adjoin White directly. If W G7–G8 runs into B G9, **W H9** hanes around G9; W H8 lets B H9 spoil the base. Mirror: W C8 vs B C9 → **B9**! Rotated: W H3 vs B J3 → **J2**. Turn ALONG the first line toward the corner. With W F7/G7 and W H8 atop B H7, preempt **J8** (mirror: W B8 above B B7 → **A8**). If B reaches J8 then wedges G8 between G7/H8, try W F8 to seal the wedge, not just crawling on row 9.
