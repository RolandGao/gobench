# Track 3.5: opening book

Compute an opening book that makes one KataGo bot beat a stronger KataGo bot as often as possible. Your only output is /workspace/book.txt. When this learning period ends, the book is cut to its first 3000 moves, counted as described below, and frozen. Nothing else in your workspace is kept.

## Evaluation
The student is the ladder player kata1-b6c96-s16525312-d2925067 (2241 Elo). Its opponent is kata1-b6c96-s22371328-d3612586 (2603 Elo). They play 1000 complete 9x9 games, the student Black in half and White in half, under Tromp-Taylor rules with komi 7 and positional superko. A win scores 1, a draw 0.5 and a loss 0; your result is the student's total. No language model plays or is consulted.

Each bot is one KataGo process:

```
/opt/katago/squashfs-root/AppRun gtp -model /opt/katago-networks/<network file> -config /opt/katago/default_gtp.cfg -override-config maxVisits=1,numSearchThreads=1,chosenMoveTemperatureEarly=0.5,chosenMoveTemperature=0.1
```

It is sent `boardsize 9`, `komi 7` and `kata-set-rules tromp-taylor`, told the other side's moves with `play`, and asked for its own with `genmove`. So a move is one neural-network evaluation, sampled from the policy with a temperature that falls during the game: games between the same two bots differ. Either bot may resign.

Whenever it is the student's turn and the game so far is in your book, the student plays the book's move instead of its own; otherwise it plays its own move. A line must match from the first move, so a game that has left the book does not return to it. The opponent never sees the book.

## The book
A book is text. A line is the student's color, B or W, then moves from the empty board, Black first, as vertices (E5) or `pass`:

```
B E5 C4 D4 C3 C5
W E5 C5 E3 E7
```

On a B line the odd moves are the student's: the book plays them. The even moves are opponent moves the book has an answer to. A W line is the reverse. Text after `#` is a comment.

A game is in the book while some symmetry of the board (the eight rotations and mirror images) maps its moves, in order, onto the start of a line of the student's color; the book's next move is mapped back to the game. So one line also stands for its seven images.

The size of a book is the number of distinct moves in its two prefix trees, one per color. Every move of every line counts, the opponent's too, but a prefix shared by several lines counts once, and so do lines that are images of each other. The limit is 3000. A book over the limit keeps the moves that come first in the file. A line is kept up to its first problem: an unreadable or illegal move, or a student move where an earlier line already chose a different one. Only the first 1024 KiB of the file are read. A missing file is an empty book.

## Your environment
You have 7200 seconds of wall time. Read /arena/clock.json for a live deadline. The runtime stops at the deadline with no extra time, so save the book early and keep it updated. Python3, g++, and make are available; build tools in /workspace. There is no internet access. Your agent has exclusive use of 1 physical CPU core with 2 logical CPUs, 4096 MiB RAM, 2048 MiB writable storage and 256 processes/threads; there is no GPU or swap. Subagents are disabled.

- `/arena/bin/book-check [FILE]` reads FILE (default /workspace/book.txt) exactly as evaluation will and reports its size, the moves beyond the limit, and the lines cut short.
- KataGo and every network of the ladder are available read-only. /arena/ladder.tsv lists each ladder player with its network file, Elo and speed, and explains how the players are configured. You may use any of them, for example stronger networks as advisers.
- There is no student tool: the student is the KataGo bot above, which you can run yourself.
