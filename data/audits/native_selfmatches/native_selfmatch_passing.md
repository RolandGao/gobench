# One-visit native self-match passing audit — 2026-09-05

Each selected KataGo player played 100 native `katago match` games against an identical copy of itself. There were no cross-level pairings and no LLM opponents. Every playing move used `maxVisits=1` and one search thread. The Elo labels come from the existing calibration, not from these self-matches.

## Results

| Target Elo | Calibrated Elo | Games | Confirmed passing losses | Games affected | Approx. 95% Wilson interval |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1000 | 1011 | 100 | 1 | 1.0% | 0.2–5.4% |
| 2000 | 2002 | 100 | 24 | 24.0% | 16.7–33.2% |
| 3300 | 3293 | 100 | 0 | 0.0% | 0.0–3.7% |

A confirmed passing loss means a decisive game ended with two consecutive passes, and both independent judging networks find that removing their estimated dead stones changes the native loser into the winner under the same KataGo terminal scorer and komi, including pass-alive removal. The denominator is games, not player appearances or pass moves. Non-terminal passing mistakes, resignations, draws, and losses that become draws are outside this criterion.

The two judges checked the same 88 decisive two-pass positions. Their winner-flip classifications disagree on 0 positions. Disagreements are excluded from the confirmed count and retained in the JSON.

These ending distributions matter: a player that usually resigns or reaches automatic pass-alive termination has fewer opportunities to produce a loss through two passes. The per-game results measure the actual calibrated policies, not isolated cleanup skill conditional on reaching an identical endgame.

The closest available one-visit players were selected from [the retained native calibration](../../../log/arena_20260805_040447_246130_2072c54f/run.json). Their original temperatures were retained, so this comparison varies both network checkpoint and temperature. It does not isolate network training progress at a common temperature.

All games used 9×9, komi 7, positional superko, area scoring, no group tax, legal multi-stone suicide, no button, and a 1,000-move cap. Resignation matched the existing native calibration: threshold −0.95 for three consecutive turns. Native pass-alive termination and scoring were retained. Each level had two bot entries with identical weights and search settings, paired exclusively with one another. Two games ran concurrently per native process; search threads and Eigen threads per model were one. NN symmetry randomization was enabled.

KataGo v1.16.5, revision `ba938676d7f42d70950b3a535af2466fb642008c`, Eigen AVX2 CPU backend. Model hashes, command lines, generated configuration files, native logs, complete SGF collections, and parsed moves were saved. Native game RNG seeds were generated by KataGo; the saved games provide the exact inputs for repeating the audit.

## Independent scoring audit

Every recorded move was checked for legality including positional superko. For every decisive two-pass game, both judges replayed the full history, verified the final board, and checked that GTP final_score matched the native SGF result. Only then did the audit enable friendlyPassOk for final_status_list dead. The estimated dead stones were removed from a copied board, which was scored using KataGo’s original Tromp–Taylor terminal scorer, including its pass-alive simplification. Every original board was also rescored through this same board-setup path and required to match the native result. Literal flood-fill scores are retained separately in the JSON; they do not decide the reported winner flips. The judges each used the fixed 100-visit ownership-status search; those visits were analysis after the games and never affected a playing move.

- `kata1-b10c128-s501483520-d110698189`
- `kata1-b18c384nbt-s9761732864-d4253420187`

Dead-stone classifications are engine estimates, not life-and-death proofs or proofs that the loser could force a win against perfect defense. The two judges are different-strength KataGo networks and can share errors. The sample describes these three particular calibrated policies, and 100 games per level cannot establish the absence of rare errors.

The stronger audit resumed after saving its first 18 completed GTP responses; the remaining cases were divided across two workers. Completed cases were retained. The saved recovery protocol documents those first 18 results.
